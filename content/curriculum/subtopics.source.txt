# Each topic's rows follow topics.json subtopic order: teaching note | French example | English meaning.
[A1-01]
French has 26 letters. Spell names letter by letter; accents are written marks, not extra alphabet letters.|Mon prénom s'écrit L-I-N-A.|My first name is spelled L-I-N-A.
Final consonants are often silent: the s in Paris is silent. Pronunciation cannot be predicted from English spelling.|J'habite à Paris.|I live in Paris.
Accents belong to French spelling: é, è and ê mark different vowel histories or sounds; ç makes c soft before a, o or u.|Le garçon prend un café.|The boy is having a coffee.
Use bonjour during the day and bonsoir in the evening; salut is informal.|Bonsoir, madame !|Good evening, madam!
Give your name with je m'appelle and ask another person's name with comment tu t'appelles.|Je m'appelle Nora, et toi ?|My name is Nora, and you?
Use tu for one familiar person and vous for a group or one person addressed politely; the verb changes too.|Vous habitez ici, monsieur ?|Do you live here, sir?
[A1-02]
In a simple statement, the subject usually comes before the verb and its object.|Lina regarde le film.|Lina is watching the film.
A French statement normally needs an expressed subject, even when the verb ending suggests who acts.|Nous travaillons ici.|We work here.
Use c'est before a singular identifying noun phrase and ce sont before a plural one.|Ce sont des amis.|They are friends.
A short complete sentence still needs a subject and a conjugated verb.|Le train arrive.|The train is arriving.
[A1-03]
Je means I and becomes j' before a vowel sound or silent h.|J'écoute la radio.|I am listening to the radio.
Tu addresses one person informally; regular -er verbs commonly end in -es with tu.|Tu parles français.|You speak French.
Il and elle refer to masculine and feminine subjects; on often means we in everyday speech but takes a singular verb.|On mange ensemble.|We eat together.
Nous means we and commonly takes a verb ending in -ons.|Nous habitons à Lyon.|We live in Lyon.
Vous means plural you or polite singular you and commonly takes a verb ending in -ez.|Vous cherchez la gare.|You are looking for the station.
Ils refers to a masculine or mixed group; elles to an all-feminine group. Both take third-person plural verbs.|Elles regardent les photos.|They are looking at the photos.
[A1-04]
Learn each noun with its article: un livre is masculine and une table is feminine; grammatical gender is not physical sex.|Une table et un livre.|A table and a book.
Nouns ending in -tion are usually feminine, but endings are clues rather than guarantees.|La station est petite.|The station is small.
Some nouns for people change form with gender, while objects keep their grammatical gender.|Mon frère est étudiant.|My brother is a student.
[A1-05]
Le, la, l' and les identify definite nouns; l' precedes a vowel sound or silent h.|J'aime la musique.|I like music.
Un, une and des introduce an unspecified singular or plural noun.|Elle achète une pomme.|She is buying an apple.
À plus le becomes au and à plus les becomes aux; de plus le becomes du and de plus les becomes des.|Je vais au marché.|I am going to the market.
Choose an indefinite article to introduce an item and a definite article when referring to that known item.|J'ai un chat. Le chat dort.|I have a cat. The cat is sleeping.
[A1-06]
Most French nouns form the plural by adding a written s, often without changing pronunciation.|Les livres sont ici.|The books are here.
Plural definite nouns use les; unspecified plural nouns usually use des.|J'achète des oranges.|I am buying oranges.
Many nouns ending in -eau form their plural with x.|Les bateaux arrivent.|The boats are arriving.
Some plural forms must be learned individually: un œil becomes des yeux.|Elle a les yeux verts.|She has green eyes.
[A1-07]
Conjugating changes a verb to match its subject and tense; the infinitive is its dictionary form.|Je parle et nous parlons.|I speak and we speak.
For regular -er verbs, remove -er and add -e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez or -ent.|Nous regardons un film.|We are watching a film.
Regular finir-type -ir verbs use -issons with nous; regular vendre-type -re verbs use -ons.|Nous finissons le travail.|We are finishing the work.
Choose the verb form from the grammatical subject, even when another noun is nearby.|Les enfants parlent français.|The children speak French.
[A1-08]
Être describes identity or state: je suis, tu es, il est, nous sommes, vous êtes, ils sont.|Je suis professeur.|I am a teacher.
Avoir expresses possession and age; French says to have a number of years.|Elle a vingt ans.|She is twenty years old.
Aller describes going: je vais, tu vas, il va, nous allons, vous allez, ils vont.|Nous allons à la gare.|We are going to the station.
Faire means to do or make and occurs in many activity expressions.|Il fait du sport.|He does sport.
Venir means to come; venir de plus a place gives someone's origin.|Je viens de Lille.|I come from Lille.
Prendre means to take and is used for transport and ordering food.|Vous prenez le bus.|You take the bus.
Pouvoir plus an infinitive expresses ability or permission.|Tu peux entrer.|You can come in.
Vouloir plus an infinitive expresses a wish or intention.|Je veux apprendre.|I want to learn.
[A1-09]
An adjective agrees with the noun it describes; a written e often marks the feminine and s the plural.|Les portes sont ouvertes.|The doors are open.
Most descriptive adjectives follow the noun, but common ones such as petit and grand often precede it.|Elle a une petite voiture rouge.|She has a small red car.
Most simple colour adjectives agree, but colours derived from nouns such as orange are usually invariable.|Les chaussures sont bleues.|The shoes are blue.
Nationality adjectives agree with their noun and normally have a lowercase initial in French.|Elles sont canadiennes.|They are Canadian.
Use être plus an adjective to describe a person or thing, checking agreement.|Cette maison est grande.|This house is big.
[A1-10]
Ne comes before a conjugated verb and pas after it; ne becomes n' before a vowel sound.|Je ne comprends pas.|I do not understand.
Ne...jamais means never; jamais replaces pas.|Elle ne fume jamais.|She never smokes.
Ne...plus means no longer or not any more and describes a change from before.|Nous ne travaillons plus ici.|We no longer work here.
A short negative reply can begin non, followed by a complete negative sentence.|Non, je ne suis pas prêt.|No, I am not ready.
[A1-11]
In familiar spoken French, a statement can become a yes/no question through rising intonation.|Tu viens demain ?|Are you coming tomorrow?
Est-ce que introduces a question without inverting the subject and verb.|Est-ce que vous aimez le thé ?|Do you like tea?
Où asks where, quand asks when, pourquoi asks why and comment asks how.|Où est la bibliothèque ?|Where is the library?
In a simple inverted question, a hyphen joins the conjugated verb to its subject pronoun.|Parlez-vous anglais ?|Do you speak English?
[A1-12]
Numbers quantify nouns; French generally places the number before the noun.|Je voudrais trois pommes.|I would like three apples.
Give a date with le plus a day number and month; use premier for the first day.|Nous sommes le deux mai.|It is the second of May.
Use il est with clock time; heure agrees with the number except at une heure.|Il est huit heures.|It is eight o'clock.
Family words often take possessives that agree with the relative's noun, not the speaker.|Ma sœur habite ici.|My sister lives here.
State many professions after être without an indefinite article.|Mon père est médecin.|My father is a doctor.
Use du, de la or de l' for an unspecified quantity of food.|Je mange du pain.|I eat bread.
Combien coûte asks the price of one item; combien coûtent asks about several.|Combien coûte cette veste ?|How much does this jacket cost?
Learn common place nouns with their articles and use à to indicate being at a place.|La pharmacie est près de la gare.|The pharmacy is near the station.
Many weather expressions use il fait; rain uses il pleut.|Il fait froid aujourd'hui.|It is cold today.
[A1-13]
A reflexive verb uses a pronoun matching its subject: me, te, se, nous, vous or se.|Je me lève tôt.|I get up early.
Describe routines with present-tense verbs and time expressions.|Je prends le petit déjeuner à sept heures.|I have breakfast at seven o'clock.
Frequency words such as souvent and toujours commonly follow a simple conjugated verb.|Elle marche souvent.|She often walks.
Jouer à introduces many games; jouer de introduces musical instruments.|Il joue au tennis.|He plays tennis.
Aimer, adorer and préférer can introduce an infinitive to describe a preferred activity.|Je préfère lire.|I prefer reading.
[A1-14]
À locates someone in a city or introduces a destination; it can contract with a following article.|Nous sommes à Montréal.|We are in Montreal.
De can express origin or link a possessed noun to its owner.|Le sac de Léa est bleu.|Léa's bag is blue.
Use en with feminine country names and masculine countries starting with a vowel.|Elle habite en France.|She lives in France.
Chez locates something at a person's home or professional premises.|Je vais chez le dentiste.|I am going to the dentist.
Location phrases such as devant and derrière describe one thing relative to another.|Le vélo est devant la maison.|The bicycle is in front of the house.
Tournez à droite and allez tout droit give simple directions.|Tournez à gauche après la banque.|Turn left after the bank.
Il y a introduces the existence or presence of something.|Il y a un parc ici.|There is a park here.
A possessive agrees with the possessed noun: mon sac, ma voiture, mes clés.|Mes clés sont sur la table.|My keys are on the table.
Et adds information, mais marks a contrast and parce que gives a reason.|Je reste ici parce qu'il pleut.|I am staying here because it is raining.
[A1-15]
A short introduction can combine name, origin and occupation in separate present-tense sentences.|Je m'appelle Adam. Je suis étudiant.|My name is Adam. I am a student.
Je voudrais is a polite way to order food or a drink.|Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.|I would like a coffee, please.
In a shop, asking for an item with vous avez is more useful than naming the item alone.|Vous avez cette chemise en bleu ?|Do you have this shirt in blue?
A brief message states the essential information and can end with à bientôt.|Salut, je suis à la gare. À bientôt !|Hi, I am at the station. See you soon!
The near future uses a present form of aller followed by an infinitive.|Nous allons visiter Paris.|We are going to visit Paris.
In a spoken exchange, attend to the question word before choosing a relevant answer; où asks for a place.|Où habites-tu ? J'habite à Nice.|Where do you live? I live in Nice.
[A2-01]
Regular present-tense endings differ by verb family: finir adds -iss- in plural forms, while vendre does not.|Nous vendons des livres et finissons à dix-huit heures.|We sell books and finish at six in the evening.
Some -er verbs change spelling to preserve a sound: manger becomes nous mangeons and commencer becomes nous commençons.|Nous commençons le cours.|We are starting the class.
High-frequency irregular verbs have stems that must be learned: prendre gives nous prenons and ils prennent.|Ils prennent le métro.|They take the metro.
The present can describe a habit or an action happening now; a time expression clarifies the intended meaning.|D'habitude, elle travaille chez elle.|She usually works from home.
[A2-02]
The passé composé combines a present auxiliary with a past participle; most verbs use avoir, while a smaller group uses être.|Elle est arrivée et a téléphoné.|She arrived and phoned.
Regular past participles end in -é for -er verbs, -i for finir-type verbs and -u for vendre-type verbs.|Nous avons vendu la voiture.|We sold the car.
With être, the past participle usually agrees with the subject in gender and number.|Mes sœurs sont parties.|My sisters left.
In a compound tense, ne and pas surround the auxiliary, not the past participle.|Je n'ai pas vu Paul.|I did not see Paul.
A passé composé question can begin with est-ce que, keeping the auxiliary before the participle.|Est-ce que tu as réservé ?|Have you booked?
[A2-03]
Build the imparfait from the nous present stem without -ons, then add -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez or -aient; être uses ét-.|Nous habitions près du lac.|We used to live near the lake.
The imparfait describes repeated past habits without presenting each repetition as a completed event.|Chaque été, je nageais dans la rivière.|Every summer, I used to swim in the river.
Use the imparfait for background conditions such as weather, appearance or an ongoing situation.|Il faisait chaud et les rues étaient calmes.|It was hot and the streets were quiet.
The imparfait sets an ongoing background; the passé composé presents an event against it.|Je lisais quand le téléphone a sonné.|I was reading when the phone rang.
[A2-04]
The near future uses aller in the present plus an infinitive; only aller is conjugated.|Elle va changer de travail.|She is going to change jobs.
The futur simple adds -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez or -ont to a future stem, often the infinitive.|Nous partirons demain.|We will leave tomorrow.
Use a future form with a time expression to make the timing of a plan explicit.|La semaine prochaine, je vais visiter ma famille.|Next week, I am going to visit my family.
[A2-05]
Reflexive pronouns agree with the subject and precede a conjugated present-tense verb.|Vous vous réveillez à quelle heure ?|What time do you wake up?
A plural reflexive pronoun can express a reciprocal action when people act on each other.|Ils se téléphonent chaque semaine.|They phone each other every week.
Reflexive verbs use être in the passé composé, with the reflexive pronoun before the auxiliary.|Elle s'est levée à huit heures.|She got up at eight o'clock.
Reflexive participle agreement depends on the pronoun's role: no agreement when se is indirect, as in se parler.|Elles se sont parlé hier.|They spoke to each other yesterday.
[A2-06]
Le, la and les replace direct objects without a preposition and come before the conjugated verb.|Je vois la gare. Je la vois.|I see the station. I see it.
Lui and leur replace indirect objects referring to people after verbs such as parler à.|Nous parlons à Léa. Nous lui parlons.|We are talking to Léa. We are talking to her.
With a conjugated verb followed by an infinitive, an object pronoun usually goes before the infinitive it belongs to.|Je vais les appeler.|I am going to call them.
[A2-07]
Compare an adjective with plus, moins or aussi followed by que.|Ce train est plus rapide que le bus.|This train is faster than the bus.
Use a definite article with le plus or le moins to identify an extreme within a group.|C'est le plus petit village de la région.|It is the smallest village in the region.
Meilleur compares bon and describes a noun; mieux compares bien and usually describes an action.|Ce pain est meilleur, mais elle cuisine mieux.|This bread is better, but she cooks better.
[A2-08]
Many adverbs form from the feminine adjective plus -ment, as in lente becoming lentement.|Elle parle lentement.|She speaks slowly.
Degree adverbs such as très and assez modify an adjective or another adverb.|Cette soupe est assez chaude.|This soup is hot enough.
Frequency adverbs describe how often something happens; jamais is normally paired with ne in formal writing.|Nous allons rarement au cinéma.|We rarely go to the cinema.
Short common adverbs often sit between the auxiliary and participle in compound tenses.|J'ai bien compris la question.|I understood the question well.
[A2-09]
Possessives agree with the possessed noun: leur voiture is singular and leurs voitures is plural.|Leurs enfants sont à l'école.|Their children are at school.
Demonstratives identify a noun: ce, cet, cette and ces; cet precedes a masculine vowel sound or silent h.|Cet appartement est lumineux.|This apartment is bright.
Stressed pronouns follow prepositions and can add emphasis: moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles.|Tu viens avec moi ?|Are you coming with me?
Y often replaces à plus a place; en replaces de plus a noun or an indefinite quantity.|Tu vas à la piscine ? Oui, j'y vais.|Are you going to the swimming pool? Yes, I am going there.
[A2-10]
Movement prepositions specify a path: vers indicates a direction and jusqu'à an endpoint.|Marchez jusqu'au pont.|Walk as far as the bridge.
Use à for cities, en for most feminine countries, au for masculine consonant-initial countries and aux for plural countries.|Ils vont au Canada.|They are going to Canada.
Directions often use the vous imperative followed by a landmark or a distance.|Continuez tout droit jusqu'au feu.|Continue straight on as far as the traffic light.
For transport, common expressions include en train, en voiture, à vélo and à pied.|Nous voyageons en train.|We travel by train.
[A2-11]
Quel agrees with the noun it accompanies: quel train, quelle rue, quels billets and quelles dates.|Quelle heure vous convient ?|What time suits you?
Rien means nothing, personne nobody and plus no longer; do not add pas to these negative patterns.|Je ne connais personne ici.|I do not know anyone here.
[A2-12]
A shopping request can specify an item, a size and a preferred alternative.|Je cherche une veste en taille moyenne.|I am looking for a medium-sized jacket.
When travelling, ask the departure time or destination with the appropriate question word.|À quelle heure part le prochain train ?|What time does the next train leave?
Propose an appointment using a date or time and ask whether it suits the other person.|Est-ce que mardi à dix heures vous convient ?|Does Tuesday at ten o'clock suit you?
At a restaurant, distinguish an order from a request for the bill.|Pourriez-vous nous apporter l'addition ?|Could you bring us the bill?
A polite request for help names the action needed after pouvez-vous or pourriez-vous.|Pouvez-vous m'aider à remplir ce formulaire ?|Can you help me fill in this form?
[A2-13]
Use the imparfait to describe a remembered habitual situation and a time expression to locate it.|Quand j'étais enfant, je jouais dans ce parc.|When I was a child, I used to play in this park.
A holiday account commonly uses passé composé for completed activities and destinations.|Nous avons passé une semaine à la mer.|We spent a week by the sea.
D'abord, ensuite and enfin make the order of past events clear.|D'abord, nous avons visité le musée, puis nous avons déjeuné.|First, we visited the museum, then we had lunch.
Describe a past emotional state with être in the imparfait, checking adjective agreement.|J'étais contente de revoir mes amis.|I was happy to see my friends again.
[A2-14]
Préférer plus an infinitive states a preference; use à to compare two nouns.|Je préfère le thé au café.|I prefer tea to coffee.
Aussi...que expresses equality; distinguish it from plus...que and moins...que.|Ce quartier est aussi calme que le nôtre.|This neighbourhood is as quiet as ours.
Parce que introduces a clause explaining a personal reason.|Je prends le vélo parce que c'est pratique.|I take my bike because it is convenient.
On pourrait plus an infinitive offers a suggestion without presenting it as an order.|On pourrait dîner ensemble samedi.|We could have dinner together on Saturday.
[A2-15]
Use et to add, mais to contrast and donc to connect a consequence to its cause.|Il pleut, donc nous restons à la maison.|It is raining, so we are staying at home.
A short email needs a greeting, a clear reason for writing and an appropriate closing.|Bonjour Léa, je confirme notre rendez-vous de demain.|Hello Léa, I confirm our appointment tomorrow.
Keep a narrative understandable by linking events rather than listing unrelated sentences.|J'ai raté le bus, alors je suis arrivé en retard.|I missed the bus, so I arrived late.
A dialogue reply should answer the previous speaker's actual request or question.|Tu es libre ce soir ? Oui, après dix-neuf heures.|Are you free tonight? Yes, after seven o'clock.
In listening, identify who, where and when before trying to interpret every individual word.|Le rendez-vous est déplacé à vendredi matin.|The appointment has been moved to Friday morning.
[B1-01]
Choose passé composé for a bounded event and imparfait for an ongoing state or habitual background, not simply for short versus long duration.|Pendant que je préparais le dîner, Léa est arrivée.|While I was preparing dinner, Léa arrived.
Past sequencing expressions order completed events and show whether they overlap or follow one another.|Il a fermé la porte, puis il est parti.|He closed the door, then he left.
An imparfait background can describe the setting before the main event begins.|La ville dormait quand nous sommes arrivés.|The city was asleep when we arrived.
[B1-02]
The future uses stems such as ser-, aur-, ir- and fer- with the same endings as regular stems.|Demain, nous aurons plus de temps.|Tomorrow, we will have more time.
The futur simple can predict a future situation without presenting it as an immediate plan.|Les températures augmenteront cette semaine.|Temperatures will rise this week.
Use the future first person to make a promise and specify the action promised.|Je te rappellerai dès mon arrivée.|I will call you back as soon as I arrive.
A future plan may include a time clause; French uses the future after quand for a future event.|Quand je terminerai mes études, je chercherai un emploi.|When I finish my studies, I will look for a job.
[B1-03]
The conditional softens a request; pourriez-vous is less direct than pouvez-vous.|Pourriez-vous fermer la fenêtre ?|Could you close the window?
Conditional devoir offers advice rather than the stronger obligation of present-tense devoir.|Tu devrais prendre un peu de repos.|You should get some rest.
J'aimerais plus an infinitive expresses a wish without asserting that it will happen.|J'aimerais apprendre à conduire.|I would like to learn to drive.
A conditional result can depend on a hypothetical condition expressed with si plus imparfait.|Avec plus de temps, nous voyagerions davantage.|With more time, we would travel more.
[B1-04]
For an open future condition, use si plus present and a future, present or imperative result; do not put the future after si.|Si tu viens, nous préparerons un repas.|If you come, we will prepare a meal.
Si plus imparfait introduces a hypothetical present condition, typically followed by a conditional result.|Si j'avais une voiture, je viendrais te chercher.|If I had a car, I would come and pick you up.
The result clause expresses what follows from the condition; its tense reflects whether the situation is open or hypothetical.|Si vous avez besoin d'aide, appelez-moi.|If you need help, call me.
[B1-05]
Parce que supplies a reason that answers an explicit or implied why question.|Je suis rentré parce que j'étais fatigué.|I went home because I was tired.
Puisque introduces a reason presented as already known or accepted in the exchange.|Puisque tu es ici, aidons Léa.|Since you are here, let us help Léa.
Car adds an explanatory reason in written or relatively formal discourse and normally follows the claim.|La route est fermée, car des travaux sont en cours.|The road is closed, because work is under way.
Donc marks a conclusion or consequence; the second proposition must follow logically from the first.|Le dernier bus est parti, donc nous prendrons un taxi.|The last bus has left, so we will take a taxi.
Alors can indicate a result or a next step; context distinguishes it from its temporal meaning then.|Il n'y avait plus de pain, alors j'en ai acheté.|There was no bread left, so I bought some.
Grâce à introduces a cause evaluated positively and is followed by a noun phrase.|Grâce à ton aide, j'ai réussi.|Thanks to your help, I succeeded.
À cause de introduces a cause usually viewed negatively and takes a noun phrase rather than a finite clause.|Le match est annulé à cause de la pluie.|The match is cancelled because of the rain.
[B1-06]
À mon avis frames a statement as the speaker's perspective rather than an uncontested fact.|À mon avis, cette solution est plus simple.|In my opinion, this solution is simpler.
Disagree with a claim respectfully by identifying the point of difference rather than rejecting the person.|Je comprends ton avis, mais je ne partage pas ta conclusion.|I understand your view, but I do not share your conclusion.
Support an opinion with a reason that explains the claim instead of merely repeating it.|Je recommande ce quartier parce qu'il est bien desservi.|I recommend this neighbourhood because it has good transport links.
[B1-07]
Qui replaces the subject of a relative clause, whether that subject is a person or a thing.|Le train qui arrive vient de Nantes.|The train that is arriving comes from Nantes.
Que replaces a direct object in a relative clause and becomes qu' before a vowel sound.|Le livre que tu lis est passionnant.|The book you are reading is fascinating.
Où introduces a relative clause locating a place or a time.|Voici le village où j'ai grandi.|Here is the village where I grew up.
Dont replaces a complement introduced by de; avoid repeating the de complement in the relative clause.|C'est le projet dont je t'ai parlé.|It is the project I told you about.
A relative clause connects information to a specific noun; choose its pronoun from the noun's role inside that clause.|J'ai une voisine qui travaille à l'hôpital.|I have a neighbour who works at the hospital.
[B1-08]
Identify the verb's construction before replacing its object: voir quelqu'un is direct, parler à quelqu'un indirect.|Je les connais et je leur parle souvent.|I know them and often talk to them.
En replaces de complements and quantities; retain an explicit number when stating how many.|Tu as des frères ? Oui, j'en ai deux.|Do you have brothers? Yes, I have two.
Before most conjugated verbs, direct le/la/les precede indirect lui/leur.|Je le lui donne.|I am giving it to him.
[B1-09]
Il faut que introduces a finite clause in the subjunctive to express necessity.|Il faut que tu sois à l'heure.|You need to be on time.
Vouloir que introduces a desired action by another subject and normally takes the subjunctive.|Je veux que vous veniez demain.|I want you to come tomorrow.
Use a subjunctive clause after expressions of necessity such as il est nécessaire que.|Il est nécessaire que nous partions tôt.|It is necessary for us to leave early.
An emotional reaction expressed with être content que normally introduces the subjunctive.|Je suis heureuse que tu ailles mieux.|I am happy that you are feeling better.
Learn common irregular subjunctives in context: être gives sois, avoir aie and aller aille.|Bien qu'il soit tard, nous continuons.|Although it is late, we are continuing.
[B1-10]
Report a statement with dire que and a yes/no question with demander si; indirect questions do not keep est-ce que.|Elle dit qu'elle est prête et demande si nous venons.|She says she is ready and asks whether we are coming.
With a present reporting verb, the reported tense often stays unchanged; past reporting can shift the temporal viewpoint.|Il disait qu'il travaillait ce jour-là.|He was saying that he was working that day.
[B1-11]
Pour plus an infinitive gives a purpose when the subject of both actions is the same.|Je révise pour réussir l'examen.|I am revising to pass the exam.
Afin de introduces an intended goal in a more formal style and takes an infinitive.|Nous écrivons afin de confirmer la réservation.|We are writing to confirm the booking.
Cependant marks a contrast between two propositions without denying the first.|Le trajet est long. Cependant, il est agréable.|The journey is long. However, it is pleasant.
Pourtant marks a result contrary to what the preceding statement would lead one to expect.|Il avait étudié, pourtant il a échoué.|He had studied, yet he failed.
Même si introduces a concession and normally takes the indicative, not the subjunctive.|Même s'il pleut, nous sortirons.|Even if it rains, we will go out.
Concession acknowledges a potentially opposing fact while maintaining the main claim.|Bien que ce soit difficile, elle persévère.|Although it is difficult, she perseveres.
[B1-12]
A story establishes a situation, introduces a change and explains the outcome; tense choices mark these roles.|Tout était calme, puis nous avons entendu un cri.|Everything was quiet, then we heard a shout.
Auparavant, ensuite and finalement locate events in a narrative timeline.|Après le repas, nous sommes retournés au bureau.|After the meal, we returned to the office.
Relevant details clarify people, places or actions rather than adding unrelated description.|La porte, qui était entrouverte, donnait sur le jardin.|The door, which was ajar, opened onto the garden.
An experience account links what happened to its effect on the speaker.|Ce voyage m'a appris à être plus patient.|That trip taught me to be more patient.
[B1-13]
Recommander de plus an infinitive gives advice; the recipient can be introduced with an indirect object.|Je vous recommande de réserver à l'avance.|I recommend that you book in advance.
Devoir states an obligation, while il faut expresses a general requirement.|Vous devez présenter une pièce d'identité.|You must show identification.
A useful solution identifies a feasible action and connects it to the problem it addresses.|Pour réduire les frais, nous pourrions partager le trajet.|To reduce costs, we could share the journey.
Negotiation combines a condition, an offer and room for the other person's response.|Je peux accepter si nous modifions la date.|I can agree if we change the date.
[B1-14]
Explain a travel problem by stating what went wrong and the practical help required.|Mon train a été annulé. Puis-je changer mon billet ?|My train was cancelled. Can I change my ticket?
In a work exchange, make the task, responsible person and deadline explicit.|Je vous enverrai le dossier avant vendredi.|I will send you the file before Friday.
Describe an educational choice by connecting a course or programme to its purpose.|J'ai choisi cette formation pour améliorer mes compétences.|I chose this course to improve my skills.
